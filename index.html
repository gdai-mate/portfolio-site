Sound familiar

You started this to be your own boss.

Somewhere along the way the business started bossing you.

Quotes get written on the couch at nine. Invoices stack up until Sunday arvo. The phone rings while you're halfway up a ladder, and that job goes to whoever picked up first. Your best worker asks the same question for the third time this month, because the answer only lives in your head.

That's what happens when every system in a business lives with the owner. Everything runs through you, so everything waits on you.

You should get to be great at your trade and still have a life outside it.

Story

I've been the one with a plate in each hand.

I walked into a cafe on Straddie as a worker. A year and a half later I was running it, and it had gone from $7K a week to $2.2M a year, five staff to thirty-five.

The first stretch was seventy-hour weeks on the floor, chasing my tail like everyone else. What turned it around was the unglamorous stuff. Tighter systems, and properly backing the people running them.

When AI hit the mainstream in 2022 all I could see was hours. The nights owners lose to admin when they should be home. So I taught myself to build it, two years of sixteen-hour days and no degree. Everything I'd put in your business runs my own days first.
